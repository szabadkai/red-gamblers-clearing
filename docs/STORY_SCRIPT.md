# The empty letter

Grandmother’s letter arrives without a trace of ink. Red can feel where the nib tore the paper. Mother wraps it around Grandmother’s deck to keep it flat and tucks it into the basket with the loaf and medicine. Three things that fit in a child’s hands carry the story: paper for a warning, bread for strangers, cards for a game Grandmother still owes her.

## The way home

Grandmother pressed hard enough to leave a message after the Wolf licked away her ink. She slipped the letter through his teeth at the family letterbox. Red cuts the three knots, follows the bell to the opening on the right, and uses Grandmother’s stitch to find the seam. At the gate, Rowan and Grandmother hold the very board Rowan owes her for the back step.

Over breakfast, Red admits she fed the Wolf. “I’d have given him a slice too,” Grandmother says. Mother puts the kettle on. When Grandmother asks for the game she owes Red, Red makes room for Pip at the table.
